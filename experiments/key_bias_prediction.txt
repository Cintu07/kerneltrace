predictions for the key bias follow-up, written and committed before running it.

what is known already: in pythia-160m and 410m the giant attention offsets come from the
key bias, and the big part of that bias sits in the dimensions that rotary embeddings do
not touch. there it adds the same number to every key's score, so softmax removes it and
it gets no gradient in exact arithmetic. zeroing that part should be an exact no-op.

1. zeroing the non-rotary key bias moves the fp32 logits by less than 0.05 anywhere in the
   vocabulary, on every prompt, on all three pythia models.
2. after zeroing it, plain bf16 (no fp32 parts at all) gets the mlp patching sweep on
   pythia-160m within 0.02 median of fp32, and the top 3 layers match fp32.
3. same for pythia-410m.
4. pythia-160m still loses the effect on more than 10 of 48 prompts in plain bf16 after
   the zeroing, because its output logits are still near 836. with the fp32 head as well,
   on none.
5. across pythia-160m's training checkpoints, the non-rotary key bias norm only grows. it
   is below 20 at step 1000 and above 200 by the end.

added before running the next two, after 1 to 4 had been checked:

6. pythia-160m in fp16 gives non-finite logits on most audit prompts, because its attention
   scores pass fp16's largest value of 65,504. with the key bias dropped, all finite.
7. on wikitext-2 test text, pythia-160m's perplexity in bf16 is more than 1% worse than fp32.
   with the key bias dropped, bf16 lands within 0.5% of fp32.

added before surveying the rest of the pythia suite, after 5 to 7 had been checked:

8. reading only the final checkpoints of pythia 70m, 1.4b, 2.8b, 6.9b and 12b, at least one
   of them besides 160m and 410m has an invariant key bias norm above 100.
9. every pythia model small enough to run here (70m and 1.4b besides the ones above) gives
   nan perplexity in fp16 if its invariant key bias norm is above 100, and finite if it is
   under 30.
