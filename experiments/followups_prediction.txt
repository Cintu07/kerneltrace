predictions for three follow-up experiments, written and committed before any of them was run.

what i know going in: santacoder's invariant key bias is 384 (query bias 7), it gives nan on all 32
fp16 windows and is fine in bf16 (+0.1%), and dropping the bias fixes fp16. bloom-560m's invariant
part is 183 but dropping it did not change its bf16 perplexity (+13.4% either way). pythia-410m's
invariant part is 132. baligar 2026 found pythia-410m-deduped's fp32 vs fp64 interaction floor is
10x gpt-2-medium's (1.25e-4 vs 1.2e-5). i have not measured pythia-410m-deduped's key bias.


A. score offsets outside pythia (scripts/score_offsets.py)
   attention scores captured at softmax in fp32 on 4 windows of 512 tokens, with and without the
   invariant key bias. "offset" is the mean score over the keys a query sees, "spread" is max minus
   min. "bf16 damage" is the total variation distance between softmax of the fp32 scores and softmax
   of the same scores rounded to bf16.

1. santacoder: the largest raw q.k (before the 1/sqrt(d) scale) is over 65,504 somewhere, so fp16
   overflows there. with the invariant bias dropped it is under 65,504 everywhere.
2. santacoder: the share of attention rows with bf16 damage over 0.1 is under 1%, which is why bf16
   was fine.
3. pythia-160m: the share of rows with bf16 damage over 0.1 is above 1% with the bias, and dropping
   the bias cuts it at least 10x.
4. bloom-560m: dropping the bias cuts its largest offset by less than half, so the bias is not what
   sets its offsets, matching the perplexity result.
5. qwen2.5-0.5b: zeroing the slow-dim key bias cuts its largest offset by at least half.


B. the baligar link (scripts/patching_floor.py)
   ioi prompts the fp32 model solves, 20 per model. residual stream patching, corrupt to clean, at
   4 depths x 2 positions (the flipped token and the last token), 28 pairs per prompt, full
   displacement. interaction q = f11 - f10 - f01 + f00 on the logit difference. fp64 is the
   reference, with the softmax and rotary kept in fp64 (hf hardcodes both to fp32). floor = median
   |q32 - q64|, as in baligar.

6. pythia-410m-deduped has an invariant key bias over 50.
7. pythia-410m-deduped: dropping the invariant key bias lowers the fp32 floor at least 3x.
8. after the drop, pythia-410m-deduped's fp32 floor is within 3x of gpt-2-medium's.
9. gpt-2-medium (invariant part 2.6): dropping it moves its fp32 floor by less than 1.5x either way.
10. the edit is exact: fp64 interactions with and without the bias agree to 1e-9 on every pair.
11. bf16 (new, baligar did not test it): on pythia-410m-deduped, more than half of the pairs have a
    bf16 error larger than the fp64 interaction itself, and dropping the bias at least halves that
    share.


C. a second task family (scripts/bf16_audit.py --task capitals)
   "the capital of france is the city of" -> paris, corrupted by swapping the country. this is factual
   recall, which leans on mid-layer mlps, where ioi leans on attention. same 4 models, same 6
   settings, same measurements as the ioi audit, up to 48 prompts the fp32 model gets right.

12. pythia-410m: bf16 changes mlp restoration by a median of at least 0.05 (sweep), and dropping the
    key bias brings that under 0.02. on ioi it was 0.107 and 0.007.
13. pythia-160m: the bf16 top-3 mlp layers differ from fp32's. with attention and the head in fp32
    they match.
14. pythia-1b and gpt-2: bf16 median sweep error under 0.03.
15. pythia-160m: the fp32 head alone makes it worse than plain bf16 (higher median sweep error), as
    on ioi.

any of these can come out wrong. whatever happens goes in the readme.


added after part A ran (bloom-560m: dropping the key bias cut its largest offset from 5,848 to 191, so
prediction 4 was wrong, but 3.2% of its attention rows still move by over 0.1 in bf16), before this run.
transformers adds bloom's alibi as slope * key_position, which is slope * query_position (the same for
every key of a query, so softmax ignores it) plus slope * (key - query). the first part is another
shared offset, up to about 360 at position 511, where bf16 steps by 2.

16. bloom-560m: writing the alibi as slope * (key - query), which is exact in fp32, brings bf16
    wikitext perplexity from +13.4% to within 2% of fp32.
